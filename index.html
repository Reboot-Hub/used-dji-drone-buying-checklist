Used Drone Inspection: Evidence-Based Decision Tree

A vendor-neutral lesson for separating verified evidence from seller claims before buying a used camera drone.

Version 1.0 Updated 9 August 2026 Suggested time: 35 minutes License: CC BY 4.0

日本語：中古 DJI ドローンを買う前に見るべき7項目

What is the decision rule?

Do not treat listing photos or a short hover video as proof that a used drone is safe, transferable, or fully functional. A defensible decision checks exact identity, account and controller status, battery condition, structure, imaging, sensors, flight warnings, repair history, and the complete kit. Any critical point that cannot be verified remains unresolved risk.

Learning objectives

After completing this module, learners should be able to:

Identify
Confirm the exact aircraft variant, controller, batteries, storage features, and included accessories.
Classify evidence
Separate information that is verified, seller-claimed, or still unresolved.
Recognize risk
Spot ownership, battery, structural, imaging, sensor, and repair-history warning signs.
Document a decision
Choose proceed, risk-adjust, specialist inspection, or stop, and explain why.

Use three evidence labels

VerifiedDirectly observed, shown in an unedited screen or video, or supported by a matching record.
Seller-claimedStated by the seller but not independently demonstrated.
UnresolvedMissing, contradictory, inaccessible, or impossible to test safely.

A confident statement is not the same as verified evidence. Record the label beside every material claim.

Six-step inspection decision tree

  1. Confirm the exact identity

    Match the model label, aircraft shell, camera system, storage features, app device screen, controller, and listed kit. Similar model names can hide materially different hardware.

    PassAll identifiers and features agree.
    PauseA photo or identifier is missing.
    StopThe label, shell, app, or camera system conflicts.

  2. Resolve ownership and controller status

    Ask for the device-management or account screen needed to show current binding status. Complete any legitimate ownership transfer before payment; do not rely on a promise to fix access later.

    PassTransfer status is demonstrated and repeatable.
    PauseThe seller needs time to retrieve the record.
    StopThe seller refuses, minimizes, or cannot resolve binding.

  3. Screen every battery without charging a suspect pack

    Inspect each battery for swelling, deformation, leakage, impact damage, damaged contacts, abnormal odor, or inconsistent identification. Record available cycle and status information. Isolate a damaged pack and seek qualified guidance.

    PassEach pack is identifiable and shows no visible damage.
    PauseStatus data is unavailable or packs differ unexpectedly.
    StopAny pack is swollen, leaking, hot, damaged, or abnormal.

  4. Inspect structure, motors, gimbal, camera, sensors, and ports

    With power safely removed where appropriate, compare arm gaps, fasteners, motor resistance, propeller seats, sensor windows, ports, gimbal alignment, lens condition, and signs of opening, impact, heat, corrosion, or liquid exposure.

    PassCondition is consistent and no critical anomaly appears.
    PauseWear or prior opening needs explanation.
    StopCracks, corrosion, burnt odor, severe play, or concealed repair signs appear.

  5. Run a controlled cold start and functional check

    Use an appropriate legal test area and a competent operator. Observe startup, app warnings, gimbal initialization, live camera view, recording, controller inputs, satellite and sensor status, motor behavior, and any model-specific feature such as internal storage.

    PassRequired functions repeat without relevant warnings.
    PauseA test cannot be completed safely or legally.
    StopWarnings, intermittent faults, unstable control, or failed recording occur.

  6. Reconcile repair history, kit completeness, and final risk

    Compare seller disclosures with physical evidence. Confirm every included battery, charger, cable, controller, cover, propeller, filter, storage item, and accessory. Do not let cosmetic appearance cancel unresolved technical or ownership risk.

    ProceedCritical evidence is verified and consistent.
    Adjust or inspectNon-critical uncertainty is priced in or referred to a specialist.
    Walk awayA critical risk remains unresolved or contradictory.

Interpretation: 11-12 supports proceeding if no stop condition exists; 8-10 calls for a documented risk adjustment or specialist inspection; 0-7 indicates substantial unresolved evidence. Any critical safety or ownership stop condition overrides the score.

Practice cases and answer rationale

Case A: attractive listing, unresolved ownership

The aircraft looks clean and records video, but the seller cannot show binding status and says it can be handled after payment.

Recommended outcome: stop. Ownership transfer is critical evidence. Cosmetic and camera tests do not resolve it.

Case B: complete proof, one untested accessory

Identity, ownership, batteries, structure, camera, sensors, and controlled flight are verified. One optional filter set is missing from the kit photo.

Recommended outcome: proceed with a documented kit adjustment. The unresolved item is non-critical and can be valued separately.

Case C: strong score, damaged battery

Most categories score 2, but one included battery is visibly swollen.

Recommended outcome: stop the battery test and isolate the pack. A critical safety condition overrides the total score.

Case D: prior opening with no repair record

Fasteners show tool marks and a panel gap is uneven. The seller says the aircraft has never been repaired.

Recommended outcome: specialist inspection or stop. The physical evidence conflicts with the disclosure.

Safety and evidence boundaries

  • Do not open, repair, modify, charge, or fly equipment that appears damaged or unsafe.
  • Do not bypass account controls, geofencing, firmware safeguards, or lawful operating requirements.
  • Use a competent operator and a lawful, controlled area for any powered or flight test.
  • This module supports education and preliminary screening; it is not a substitute for a qualified inspection.
  • Model-specific manuals, local rules, and battery guidance take precedence over this general lesson.
