# Used Drone Inspection: Evidence-Based Decision Tree

**Version:** 1.0  
**Updated:** 9 August 2026  
**Suggested time:** 35 minutes  
**Audience:** Adult education, vocational training, buyer education, and independent study  
**License:** CC BY 4.0

## Quick answer

Do not treat listing photos or a short hover video as proof that a used drone is safe, transferable, or fully functional. A defensible decision checks exact identity, account and controller status, battery condition, structure, imaging, sensors, flight warnings, repair history, and the complete kit. Any critical point that cannot be verified remains unresolved risk.

## Learning objectives

After completing this module, learners should be able to:

1. Confirm the exact aircraft variant, controller, batteries, storage features, and included accessories.
2. Separate information that is verified, seller-claimed, or unresolved.
3. Recognize ownership, battery, structural, imaging, sensor, and repair-history warning signs.
4. Apply a repeatable decision tree without opening or modifying the aircraft.
5. Document one of four outcomes: proceed, proceed with a risk adjustment, request specialist inspection, or stop.

## Three evidence labels

- **Verified:** Directly observed, shown in an unedited screen or video, or supported by a matching record.
- **Seller-claimed:** Stated by the seller but not independently demonstrated.
- **Unresolved:** Missing, contradictory, inaccessible, or impossible to test safely.

## Six-step decision tree

### 1. Confirm exact identity

Match the model label, aircraft shell, camera system, storage features, app device screen, controller, and listed kit. Stop when the label, shell, app, or camera system conflicts.

### 2. Resolve ownership and controller status

Ask for the device-management or account screen needed to show current binding status. Complete any legitimate ownership transfer before payment. Stop when the seller refuses, minimizes, or cannot resolve binding.

### 3. Screen every battery

Inspect each pack for swelling, deformation, leakage, impact damage, damaged contacts, abnormal odor, or inconsistent identification. Do not charge a suspect pack. Stop when any pack is swollen, leaking, hot, damaged, or abnormal.

### 4. Inspect structure and imaging hardware

Compare arm gaps, fasteners, motor resistance, propeller seats, sensor windows, ports, gimbal alignment, lens condition, and signs of opening, impact, heat, corrosion, or liquid exposure. Escalate cracks, corrosion, burnt odor, severe play, or concealed repair signs.

### 5. Run a controlled functional check

Use an appropriate legal test area and a competent operator. Observe startup, app warnings, gimbal initialization, live camera view, recording, controller inputs, satellite and sensor status, motor behavior, and model-specific features. Stop for relevant warnings, intermittent faults, unstable control, or failed recording.

### 6. Reconcile history, kit, and final risk

Compare seller disclosures with physical evidence. Confirm every included battery, charger, cable, controller, cover, propeller, filter, storage item, and accessory. Choose proceed, risk-adjust, specialist inspection, or stop. Cosmetic appearance never cancels unresolved technical or ownership risk.

## Evidence-quality rubric

Score each category from 0 to 2:

- **2:** Material evidence is verified and consistent.
- **1:** Evidence is partial, but no critical conflict is present.
- **0:** Evidence is missing, contradictory, or unsafe to test.

Categories: identity, ownership, battery, structure, functions, and history/kit.

- **11-12:** Proceed only if no critical stop condition exists.
- **8-10:** Document a risk adjustment or request specialist inspection.
- **0-7:** Treat the evidence as substantially unresolved.

Any critical safety or ownership stop condition overrides the score.

## Practice cases and answer rationale

1. **Clean aircraft, unresolved ownership:** Stop. Cosmetic and camera evidence cannot resolve ownership transfer.
2. **Complete proof, one optional accessory missing:** Proceed with a documented kit adjustment.
3. **Strong total score, one swollen battery:** Stop the battery test and isolate the pack. Safety overrides the score.
4. **Tool marks and uneven panel gap, but seller denies repair:** Request specialist inspection or stop because physical and verbal evidence conflict.

## Safety boundaries

- Do not open, repair, modify, charge, or fly equipment that appears damaged or unsafe.
- Do not bypass account controls, geofencing, firmware safeguards, or lawful operating requirements.
- Use a competent operator and a lawful, controlled area for any powered or flight test.
- This module supports preliminary screening, not a qualified technical inspection.
- Model-specific manuals, local rules, and battery guidance take precedence.
